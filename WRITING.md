# The opening of a paper

Correct and completely proved is the baseline. The opening of a paper also does these eight things, concisely.

1. The title says the theorem as the field knows it and names whose theorem, conjecture or programme it is: "after"
   the person whose idea it carries out.
2. The abstract's first sentence says whose work this answers or starts from. The abstract then says what is proved,
   what is granted and why it matters, and last who produced and checked it.
3. Each main theorem is stated in full and then in words, with the famous statement it implies or refutes.
4. It says why anyone should care: the field, what depended on the question, what changes now.
5. It splits the credit exactly: what the source proved or sketched, and what is supplied here.
6. It gives the context: the earlier results compared by statement and by ingredients, never by merit, and the recent
   work that changed the picture, that of AI laboratories included.
7. It says what isn't proved, in the same place.
8. It explains why the proof works before it proves anything.

The model is [WB-0001](works/WB-0001.md), *A short proof of Deligne's bound for Frobenius eigenvalues, after Alpöge*.
